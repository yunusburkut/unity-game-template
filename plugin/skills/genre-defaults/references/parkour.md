# Parkour

Move fluidly through 3D spaces with vaults, wall runs, climbs and slides (Mirror's Edge, Titanfall, Jusant); the core
verb is keep moving.

**Camera (Unity):** a first-person perspective camera on the runner's head; or a perspective third-person orbit
behind a runner or climber.

## Defaults

- **course-and-clock**: A route from a start to a finish seen from the start, a run timer on screen and the best time
  on the finish screen. Why: a clear line and a time to beat make every attempt count. Break it when: the city is free
  to roam, with no route and no clock.
- **runner-vision**: Everything you can climb, vault or run along shares one bright colour, and the rest of the world
  stays plain. Why: the route is read at speed, without stopping to look. Break it when: finding the way is the puzzle,
  a climb where each hold is searched for.
- **quick-restart**: A fall puts you back at the last checkpoint at once, with the clock still running. Why: a missed
  jump costs seconds, so the hard line gets tried again. Break it when: each run is one attempt at a dangerous climb.

## Feel

- **keep-momentum**: Speed builds while you run clean; a vault, wall run, slide or a roll pressed on a hard landing
  keeps it, and running into a wall loses it. Why: a clean line is felt as rising speed. Break it when: a slow, careful
  climb where speed never matters.
- **forgiving-reach**: A ledge just out of reach is still caught, a jump pressed a moment after the edge still counts,
  and a gap almost wide enough is slipped through (numbers in game-feel). Why: a near miss becomes a save, and the run
  keeps flowing. Break it when: exact reach is the challenge.
- **speed-shows**: The field of view widens and the wind rises with speed, and a reduced-motion setting turns it down.
  Why: speed is felt without the controls changing. Break it when: the camera is far behind and the world moving past
  shows the speed.
- **body-in-view**: In first person, hands reach out on vaults and climbs and the view dips a little on landing, with a
  setting to calm it. Why: the player feels a body, not a floating camera. Break it when: the camera is behind the
  runner and the whole body shows.

## Variants

- **flowing**: speed builds and carries, wall runs chain into slides, one long line.
- **precise**: short exact jumps, quick stops, a time trial of clean inputs.
- **climber**: slow and grounded, grip that runs out, each hold chosen with care.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **What you do:** a run from start to finish against the clock (default) · a climb to the top with grip that runs out
  · deliveries across the rooftops, racing the weather.
- **Stakes:** a quick restart at checkpoints (default) · a chase with a pursuer close behind · none, roam and explore.
- **View:** first person (default) · third-person orbit from behind.
- **Feeling:** the player feels kinetic flow by chaining a vault into a wall run into a slide without losing speed,
  because each clean move carries its speed into the next.

## Platforms

- PC: WASD and mouse look, Space to jump, one key for slide or roll; vaults and wall runs trigger from movement, not
  extra keys. Touch: a left-thumb move stick, right-side drag to look, two buttons (jump, slide) *(guess)*.
- Phone: landscape only. First-person parkour on touch is hard to control; consider auto-run with swipes for the moves
  and test it early on a phone *(guess)*.
- Runs of 1-3 min fit both; PC players replay one course for a time, phone players want short checkpoints.

## Later

- A ghost of your best run to race (local).
- A grapple and swinging.
- A city in layers from the sewers to the roofs.
- Fights and guns between the moves (a movement shooter).
- Rain that makes holds slippery.
- Ropes and anchors on a climb.
- Advanced tricks kept as techniques.
- Times shared online, with a ranking (needs a server).

## In a mashup

Parkour leads movement, the camera and the route. A shooter half leads weapons and aim, and movement keeps its momentum
through the fight. A stealth half leads guards and sight, and parkour gives it rooftop routes. A racing half leads
rivals and the race, and the course stays a line to run. A survival or delivery half leads what is carried and the
weather.
