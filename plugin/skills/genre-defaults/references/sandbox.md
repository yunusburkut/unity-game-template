# Sandbox

An open space of materials and tools to make, break or play with, and no set goal; the core verb is trying an idea.

**Camera (Unity):** a first-person perspective camera, building and breaking up close (default); a perspective orbit
around a small build site; or an orthographic side view for falling sand and powders.

## Defaults

- **small-first-slice**: One small space, a few materials and tools, few readable rules. Why: a whole simulated world
  is not a first slice. Break it when: never for the first slice.
- **few-materials**: Each material has one plain property (wood burns, stone is heavy, glass shatters) and acts the
  same everywhere. Why: the player invents uses from rules they can predict. Break it when: a building toy where
  materials differ only in look.
- **try-list**: The game opens straight into the space with every tool ready and a short list of things to try,
  ticked off as they happen and never required. Why: no goal, but never a blank page. Break it when: the user wants
  set challenges, a bridge that must hold.
- **undo-and-reset**: One input undoes the last thing placed or broken, another puts the whole space back. Why:
  experiments are free, so the wild idea gets tried. Break it when: the mess is meant to stay.

## Feel

- **chain-reactions**: What you set going keeps going: fire spreads along wood, a pushed stack topples into the next,
  a spark sets off a barrel. Why: watching the setup play out is the reward. Break it when: a calm building toy.
- **breaks-with-weight**: Broken things split into pieces thrown along the hit, each material with its own crack and
  thud, and debris stays a while. Why: destruction reads as physical. Break it when: nothing breaks.
- **snap-build**: Placed pieces snap to a grid with a ghost preview and join their neighbours. Why: anyone can build
  something that looks right. Break it when: loose props to fling and stack are the toy.
- **build-then-use**: One input switches from building to using what was built, at once: the cart rolls, the trap
  goes off. Why: a creation matters when it does something. Break it when: the build is the art, a model to look at.

## Variants

- **builder**: snapping blocks, placed pieces that stay put, a tidy grid.
- **toybox**: loose physics props that roll, stack, tip and fall.
- **wrecker**: everything breaks into pieces, fire spreads, the mess is the fun.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **The toy:** blocks to build with (default) · props to fling and stack · a built place to tear down · a town that
  reacts to trouble, a crime sandbox (mostly out of reach for a first slice).
- **Goals:** none, a list of things to try (default) · set challenges, a bridge that must hold a cart · a need to
  survive, which makes it survival-crafting.
- **The space:** one small hand-made yard (default) · a generated patch of land.
- **View:** first person (default) · orbit around a build site · a side view of falling sand.
- **Feeling:** the player feels creative ownership by building something and then using it, because the pieces snap
  together easily and the thing works.

## Platforms

- PC: WASD and mouse look, left click places, right click breaks, number keys or the wheel pick the material.
- Touch: first person on touch needs a stick and a look-drag zone; the orbit or side view suits phones better, with
  tap to place and a long press to break *(guess)*. Landscape.
- Session: open-ended; a phone session is one build, PC sessions run long. Saving creations is a Later item.

## Later

- A world that keeps running out of sight
- People and police who react to trouble
- Vehicles you build and drive
- Ragdoll bodies that stumble and fall
- Buildings that collapse when their supports go
- Wires, switches and logic
- Saving creations and loading them again
- Building together with friends (needs a server)

## In a mashup

The sandbox leads materials and what the tools do to them. Survival-crafting leads needs and threats, and the
materials become recipes. A puzzle half leads goals, and checks that each challenge can be solved. Racing leads
handling when you drive what you built. A management half leads money when building costs.
