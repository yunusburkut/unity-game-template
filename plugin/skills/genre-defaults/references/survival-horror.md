# Survival horror

A weak player with scarce resources, where fleeing matters as much as fighting; the core verb is search a room while
listening. Like Resident Evil, Silent Hill or Five Nights at Freddy's.

**Camera (Unity):** a perspective third-person camera close over the shoulder; a first-person camera; fixed-angle
cameras set room by room; a top-down camera that shows only what the character sees; a fixed camera on one room
watched through switchable cameras.

## Defaults

- **scarce-supplies**: Ammo and healing are few and found by searching, and what turns up leans toward what the
  player is short of. Why: every bullet matters, and running away is a real choice. Break it when: action horror
  against hordes, with plenty to shoot.
- **small-inventory**: A few slots, shown on one screen, and a box in the safe room for the rest. Why: picking
  something up is a choice that shapes the route. Break it when: a short chase with nothing to carry.
- **safe-room-save**: A safe room that nothing follows into holds the save point and the box, and progress is kept
  only there. Why: relief after pressure, and a reason to push one more room. Break it when: the safe place is what
  turns unsafe, a house that changes as you loop it.

## Feel

- **hear-it-first**: The hunter is heard before it is seen, from its side and louder as it nears, over a low, uneasy
  hum that rises with the danger. Why: dread builds before the threat appears, and the player can act on what they
  hear. Break it when: a silent stalker is the twist, and the quiet is the warning.
- **look-costs**: The map, the inventory and a detector do not pause the game. Why: looking costs attention, so even
  a menu is tense. Break it when: a slower puzzle horror, where the inventory is a safe moment to think.
- **dark-and-light**: The dark hides the edges of the screen, and the player's light shows only what is ahead. Why:
  what cannot be seen is filled in by fear. Break it when: horror in plain daylight, the threat in full view.
- **hunter-backs-off**: One threat hunts by sight and sound, searches where it last saw the player, and backs off for
  a while after a long chase. Why: tension rises and falls instead of becoming constant noise. Break it when: no
  hunter at all; the horror is in the place.

## Variants

- **dread**: long quiet, one hunter, sounds that might be nothing.
- **action-horror**: more monsters, just enough ammo, aim that has to hold steady.
- **helpless**: no weapon at all, only hiding and running.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **The threat:** one hunter that stalks you (default) · many slow monsters to fight or avoid · something watched on
  cameras that comes closer while you look away, as in Five Nights at Freddy's.
- **Weapons:** a few, with scarce ammo (default) · none, hide and run · one that is loud and draws the hunter.
- **View:** over the shoulder (default) · through the eyes · from above, seeing only what the character sees · a room
  and its cameras.
- **Saving:** in the safe room (default) · a save costs an item · checkpoints.
- **Feeling:** the player feels psychological dread by listening for the hunter while searching a room, because
  looking costs attention and the sound comes before the sight.

## Platforms

- PC: WASD moves, the mouse looks and aims, keys open the inventory and light. Touch: a left virtual stick, a right
  drag to look, aim assist and an action button *(guess)*; the camera-watching form is taps only and fits phones best.
- Phone: landscape. Headphones carry the directional sound the genre leans on; say so on the title screen.
- Safe room to safe room is 10-20 min on PC; on a phone, add a suspend save that does not replace the safe room
  *(guess)*.

## In a mashup

Horror leads what the player can know and the pace of threats. A shooter leads aiming and gun feedback, survival
leads scarcity and the inventory, and no game stacks every mercy rule or every awareness limit by default. Co-op
leads rescue and communication, and horror the local threat. Stealth leads detection, and horror the pace. A walking
sim leads the story.
