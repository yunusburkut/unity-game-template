# Fishing

Cast, wait, hook and reel in fish, and fill a log of catches (Dredge, Ridiculous Fishing, Stardew Valley's fishing);
the core verb is reel.

**Camera (Unity):** a fixed camera on the water from the shore or a boat (orthographic for 2D, perspective for 3D); an
orthographic top-down camera to walk or sail between spots; an orthographic side view that follows the line down for a
deep drop (Ridiculous Fishing).

Research gap: the CCGS sources had three entries on the wait, the bite and the reel, and nothing on the catch log or on
selling the catch for gear. Treat every line as a hypothesis.

## Defaults

- **spots-differ**: A few spots (shallows, deep water, the rocks), each with its own fish, and some fish bite only at
  dusk or in rain. Why: where and when to cast is the player's choice. Break it when: one spot is the whole game, a
  quick reflex test.

## Feel

- **quiet-wait**: After the cast the float rests on calm water with soft sound, and the wait is never long. Why: the
  quiet makes the bite land. Break it when: frantic arcade fishing, fish leaping at the line.
- **true-bite**: Nibbles tug the float first; the real bite pulls it under with a splash, a sound and a shake that
  cannot be missed, and a missed strike is quick to retry. Why: the player watches closely instead of pressing on
  reflex. Break it when: every bite is real, a gentle one-button fishing.
- **reel-fight**: Reeling is a short contest: hold to pull, ease off when the line strains, and the fish fights back in
  bursts, harder for a big one. Why: the catch is won by skill after the wait. Break it when: the catch is one timed
  press.
- **catch-show**: A landed fish is held up with its name, size and a jingle, bigger for a new kind or a record. Why:
  every catch is a small reward, a new kind a big one. Break it when: nets haul fish by the dozen and the haul is the
  reward.

## Variants

- **calm**: long quiet waits, a forgiving reel, nothing lost.
- **arcade**: quick bites, a reflex reel, a score against the clock.
- **eerie**: night fishing pays more and risks more, and strange things bite.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **What you catch:** fish (default) · animals tracked by trails and dens (hunting) · junk and treasure among the fish.
- **Stakes:** none, a lost fish swims off (default) · a quota to fill before dark (a day clock) · a dangerous night,
  where a long trip risks the catch.
- **Progression:** a better rod and bait from selling fish (default) · a boat that reaches new waters · the log alone.
- **View:** water from the shore (default) · top-down, walking or sailing · a line dropping deep, side view.
- **Feeling:** the player feels the weight of a big catch by fighting it in on a straining line after a quiet wait,
  because the true bite is clear and the reel rewards skill.

## Platforms

- PC: mouse or one key to cast (hold for distance), hold to reel, release to ease off. Touch: the same hold and release
  on the screen through Pointer; the bite shake also fires a phone vibration.
- Phone: portrait for a deep-drop line or a single spot; landscape for a shore or a sea to sail *(guess)*.
- A few casts fit a 2-minute phone session; a PC session is a whole day or trip. Save the log after every catch.

## Later

- Many waters on a map, each with its own fish.
- A boat to sail and upgrade.
- Cooking and eating the catch.
- An aquarium or museum that shows the catch.
- Night horrors and a fraying nerve, as in Dredge.
- Fish and game that roam, feed and flee on their own.
- Weather and seasons that change what bites.
- Contests and records against other players (needs a server).

## In a mashup

Fishing leads the cast, the bite and the reel. A farming or life sim leads the day clock, and fish sell where crops
sell. Survival-crafting leads hunger, and fish are food. Horror leads the dark and what lurks in it, and fishing leads
the catch. An adventure leads the world, and fishing sits in it as a calm side activity.
