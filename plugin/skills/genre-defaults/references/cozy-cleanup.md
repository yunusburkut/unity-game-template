# Cozy cleanup

Clean, tidy, sort or decorate a space until it looks right (PowerWash Simulator, Unpacking, A Little to the Left); the
core verb is scrub or place.

**Camera (Unity):** a fixed orthographic camera that fits one room or object; a first-person perspective camera with
the tool in hand (PowerWash Simulator); or a perspective orbit around one object.

Research gap: the CCGS sources had one cleaning-sim row (PowerWash Simulator, Unpacking) and nothing on choosing among
tools per kind of dirt. Treat every line as a hypothesis.

## Defaults

- **clear-finish**: A bar fills as the mess goes, and each object or area is ticked off when done. Why: the job can be
  finished, and the player sees how far is left. Break it when: free decorating with no right answer.
- **forgiving-finish**: When an object is nearly clean the last specks clear themselves, and a hint shows a missed
  spot. Why: finishing never becomes a hunt for an invisible speck. Break it when: precision is the challenge.
- **no-pressure**: No timer and no costly move: anything placed can be picked up again, and nothing breaks. Why: the
  player settles into the task. Break it when: a timed rush to tidy before the guests arrive.

## Feel

- **every-stroke-shows**: Each stroke clears the mess under it at once, with a sound that changes as the surface comes
  clean. Why: each action visibly reduces the mess, the heart of the genre. Break it when: tidying is about where things
  go, not scrubbing.
- **snap-into-place**: An item dragged near its spot slides into it with a soft click, and things sit neatly on
  shelves. Why: placing feels tidy without fiddly aim. Break it when: a little mess is the charm.
- **done-chime**: Finishing an object flashes it, rings a small chime and ticks it off; the whole job ends on a bigger
  one. Why: each small finish rewards the way to the big one. Break it when: one quiet reveal at the end is the point.
- **before-after**: The finished space is shown before and after, or as a short replay. Why: the player sees how much
  they changed. Break it when: the mess keeps coming back, an endless tidy.

## Variants

- **scrub**: one tool, one big dirty thing, a lot of surface to clear.
- **unpack**: items from boxes to where they belong, a quiet puzzle of a life.
- **arrange**: small scenes to set just right, each with a neat answer.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **The job:** wash a dirty thing clean (default) · unpack and sort a room · set small arrangements just right ·
  decorate a room freely.
- **Stakes:** none, no timer and nothing lost (default) · a star rating for a quick or tidy finish · paid jobs that buy
  better tools.
- **View:** one room or object, fixed orthographic (default) · first person with the tool · orbit around one object.
- **Feeling:** the player feels order from chaos by watching each stroke clear the mess, because every action visibly
  reduces disorder and the finish is forgiving.

## Platforms

- PC: hold the mouse button and sweep to scrub; drag to place. Touch: the same press-and-drag through Pointer; the
  finger hides what it cleans, so offset the tool or widen its brush *(guess)*.
- Phone: portrait for one object or one shelf, landscape for a room *(guess)*.
- One object or arrangement per phone session (a few minutes); a whole room per PC session. Save progress per object.

## Later

- A life story told by what is in the boxes.
- Many rooms or jobs in a row.
- Better tools bought with job pay.
- Furniture from a shop to decorate with.
- Cleaning together with a friend on one screen.
- Dirt that comes back over time.

## In a mashup

Cleanup leads the mess, the tools and the finish. A farming or life sim leads the day, and tidying is one chore in it.
A detective game leads the clues, and cleaning turns them up. Horror leads the dread when the mess is a crime scene.
Management leads the money when the jobs are paid.
