# Survival-crafting

Gather and craft against hunger and threats, and build a base; the core verb is the gather-craft-build trip.

**Camera (Unity):** a perspective camera angled from above that follows the player (default); first person, as in
Minecraft or Subnautica; a third-person orbit behind the player, as in Valheim; or an orthographic side view dug
downward.

## Defaults

- **small-first-slice**: One small island or clearing, one night, a few materials and recipes. Why: a whole simulated
  world is not a first slice. Break it when: never for the first slice.
- **needs-in-view**: Health, hunger and one need of the setting (warmth, or air under water) sit near the player,
  fall slowly and are filled by what is gathered and cooked. Why: the player knows why the next trip matters. Break
  it when: a peaceful build mode with no needs.
- **recipes-point-ahead**: The craft list shows what can be made now, and greyed recipes show what is missing and
  where it is found. Why: the things to make give the player a direction. Break it when: recipes are found by trying
  combinations, and the discovery is the point.
- **drop-and-return**: Dying drops what was carried where you fell, and you wake at your bed; going back gets it.
  Why: death stings without erasing the run. Break it when: hard stakes, one life and the run is over.

## Feel

- **first-night**: Dusk comes soon and is felt (light dims, sounds change), and the first recipes are a fire and a
  shelter that keep the night out. Why: the open world gets one plain first purpose. Break it when: a creative mode
  with no danger.
- **gather-thunk**: Each hit on a tree or rock takes a bite with a material sound and a spray of bits, the last hit
  topples it, and the pickups fly to you. Why: the hundredth log still feels good. Break it when: gathering is from a
  menu.
- **snap-build**: Walls and floors snap to a grid with a ghost that shows their cost and whether they fit. Why: a
  shelter goes up fast and looks right. Break it when: free placement is the craft, logs laid one by one.
- **home-is-safe**: Inside walls by a fire the music softens, needs fall slower and a rested bonus shows. Why: home
  feels safe, so building one matters. Break it when: a wanderer with no base.

## Variants

- **gentle**: slow hunger, few threats, room to build.
- **harsh**: hunger that bites, a deadly night, every trip a gamble.
- **deep**: under water, air is the clock, danger from every side.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **The setting:** a wild clearing or island (default) · under water, air the main need · a side view dug downward.
- **Stakes:** drop what you carried and wake at your bed (default) · one life · none, a creative mode.
- **The threat:** night creatures (default) · cold and storms · hunger alone.
- **The world:** a small generated island (default) · a hand-made clearing.
- **View:** from above (default) · first person · orbit behind the player · a side view.
- **Feeling:** the player feels creative ownership by turning the first night's shelter into a home, because each
  build solved a real need and is made of what they gathered.

## Platforms

- PC: WASD moves, mouse aims and hits, Tab or I opens crafting, number keys for the hotbar.
- Touch: a virtual stick and tap-to-gather on the nearest thing; crafting is a full-screen list *(guess)*. Top-down
  suits phones better than first person. Landscape.
- Session: one day-night cycle per phone sitting; save at the bed. PC sessions run several days *(guess)*.

## Later

- Weather that soaks, chills and spoils gear
- Animals with food and dens that roam on their own
- Enemies that hunt by sight and sound
- Tool tiers that open new regions
- Bosses whose rewards open a new frontier
- Machines that take over the chores
- Taming and farming
- An inventory that is a grid puzzle
- Surviving together with friends (needs a server)

## In a mashup

Survival leads needs, recipes and the day-night threat. A sandbox leads materials and free building. A colony sim
leads when the player commands others instead of one hero. Horror leads the dread and the hunter, and survival leads
supplies. Farming leads crops and the day's rhythm when food is grown. An action RPG leads the fights.
