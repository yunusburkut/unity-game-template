# Walking sim

Walk through a place and find its story, with little or no challenge; the core verb is look closer. Like Firewatch,
Gone Home or What Remains of Edith Finch.

**Camera (Unity):** a first-person perspective camera; a perspective third-person camera behind a figure you can see;
a fixed-angle camera that follows.

*Thin sources:* the sources cover reveals, travel and small discoveries that chain into a mystery, not notes,
diaries, recorded voices or walk speed. Treat those lines as the model's reading.

## Defaults

- **story-trail**: The story is found in order: something wrong is in view from the start, a trail of traces leads
  on, and the last piece ends the walk on an end screen. Why: the player pieces it together, and the ending lands
  because they found their way to it. Break it when: the walk is pure mood with no story, a calm stroll.
- **lines-while-walking**: Narration and voices show as a subtitle line at the bottom while you keep walking, never in
  a box that stops you. Why: the story never stops the walk. Break it when: the story is all in objects and notes,
  with no voice at all.

## Feel

- **gentle-walk**: Walking is slow and smooth, holding an input goes a little faster, and a gentle sway of the view can
  be turned off in the settings. Why: the place is seen, not rushed, and no one feels sick. Break it when: the walk
  is a chase, a thriller where speed matters.
- **lead-the-way**: Light, paths and one landmark seen from the start pull the walk on, and the next room shows from
  this one. Why: the player never feels lost and never needs an arrow. Break it when: getting lost is the point, a
  maze or a dream.
- **pick-up-and-turn**: Things worth a look glow faintly when you look at them; picking one up brings it close to turn
  over, with its own sound. Why: the story is in the objects, and holding one makes it real. Break it when: nothing
  is touched, a walk told only by voice.
- **reveal-the-space**: A narrow way in opens onto a wide view, and the music swells at the opening. Why: the reveal
  is the walk's reward, felt before it is understood. Break it when: the whole walk is one small room.
- **footsteps-and-air**: Footsteps sound different on wood, grass and stone, and each room has its own quiet
  background sound. Why: the place feels solid and lived in with nothing on screen. Break it when: the look is
  abstract and silent on purpose.

## Variants

- **wistful**: a slow walk through a lived-in home, small objects, soft music.
- **uneasy**: rooms change a little when you turn back, sounds come from places you left, and nothing ever attacks.
- **awe**: a long walk across open land toward a huge landmark, wind and distance.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **Where:** one house, room by room (default) · open land walked toward a landmark · a place that loops and changes
  each time round · the sea floor, open above and below.
- **How the story comes:** objects and notes you find (default) · lines that play as you reach places · a voice on a
  radio you answer.
- **View:** your own eyes (default) · a figure you follow from behind.
- **Feeling:** the player feels quiet discovery by finding the last piece of a story in a place, because each object
  they held added a piece.

## Platforms

- PC: WASD walks, the mouse looks, a click picks up and the mouse turns the held object. Touch: a left virtual stick
  walks, a right-side drag looks, a tap on a glowing object picks it up *(guess)*.
- Phone: landscape.
- The whole walk is 1-2 hours on PC; on a phone, save at each room or chapter so a 10-15 min sitting can stop
  anywhere *(guess)*.

## Later

- Talking back to a voice, with choices.
- More rooms and a longer story.
- A place that changes for what you have already seen.
- A list of found things to collect.
- Sound muffled through walls and doors.
- A final action that sums up the story.
- A threat that hunts you through the place.

## In a mashup

The walking sim leads the pace, the place and the order the story is found in. A detective half leads the evidence
and what the player must work out. A puzzle half leads its rules, and the story leads what solving them means. Horror
leads the threat and the dread. A cozy or tidying half leads gentle handling, and the story leads what the objects
mean.
