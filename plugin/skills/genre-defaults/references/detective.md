# Detective

Gather clues and reason out what happened; the core verb is noticing, then naming the answer.

**Camera (Unity):** one static camera on a drawn scene, orthographic for 2D (default); a first-person perspective
camera for a room you walk through in 3D.

## Defaults

- **one-fair-case**: The first slice is one case: one scene or a few rooms, a handful of clues, one deduction, and
  it is solvable from what is shown. Why: fairness is the genre; more cases come after one works. Break it when:
  never for the first slice.
- **notebook**: Every clue found goes into a notebook, open at any time, in the words the answer needs. Why: nothing
  found is lost, and clues compare side by side. Break it when: the whole case fits on one screen.
- **fill-the-blanks**: The answer is built, not picked: fill blanks (who did what, to whom, how) from found names and
  words. Why: a right answer proves the player worked it out. Break it when: the user wants an accuse-the-suspect
  finale, one pick from a line-up.
- **hints-on-ask**: A Hint button points to where to look, never to what it means. Why: a stuck player moves on and
  the deduction stays theirs. Break it when: the user wants it hard, with no help.

## Feel

- **look-closely**: Any spot can be looked at closer; the view eases in, and a real clue chimes and flies into the
  notebook. Why: the player is the one who spots it. Break it when: all clues are testimony, nothing to look at.
- **confirm-in-groups**: The game says correct only when a few linked answers are right together, with a clear sting.
  Why: guessing one at a time fails, so a confirmation is a real eureka. Break it when: the case is one question.
- **case-retold**: The solve ends on a screen that retells what really happened, step by step, ending on who did it.
  Why: the player sees their reasoning come true. Break it when: the mystery is meant to stay open.

## Variants

- **cozy**: no clock, no penalty, a tidy case at your own pace.
- **tense**: a clock runs, a suspect may get away, and the sound tightens as time runs out.
- **loop**: the same few minutes repeat, and only what you learned carries into the next loop.

## Choices that shape the pitch

- **The case:** one scene frozen at the moment it happened, read for who did what (default) · a time loop where
  knowledge opens the way · recorded interviews searched by word.
- **How it is solved:** fill the blanks from found words (default) · accuse one suspect at the end · catch a lie by
  showing the clue that breaks it.
- **Stakes:** no penalty, answers confirm in groups (default) · a few wrong accusations, then the case goes cold ·
  a clock.
- **View:** one drawn scene (default) · walk the room in first person.
- **Feeling:** the player feels independent deduction by naming who did it from clues no one explained, because the
  game confirms only when the reasoning is right.

## Platforms

- PC: point and click to inspect, scroll or drag to look around; type or pick words for the blanks.
- Touch: tap to inspect through Pointer, pinch to zoom; blanks are filled by picking found words, never by typing
  *(guess)*. No hover, so clue spots need a tap-to-check rule. Landscape for a wide scene; portrait for a notebook-led
  case *(guess)*.
- Session: one case in 20-40 min on PC; on a phone, save after every clue and confirmation *(guess)*.

## Later

- A case that turns midway into a different problem
- Several cases and a story across them
- Suspects who answer questions, and lie
- A board of leads that links places and clues
- Revisiting a place that has changed
- A city of suspects who live their own lives
- Endings that depend on when you face the culprit

## In a mashup

The detective leads the evidence, the notebook and what counts as solved. A metroidvania or an action-adventure leads
movement and the map, and knowledge can open a door the way a tool does. A visual novel leads the talks with
suspects. A cozy half leads gentle handling, and hints never give the answer away. A walking sim leads the place and
the order it is explored in.
